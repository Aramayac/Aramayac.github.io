Linking two branches with an encrypted tunnel

Connect the Richard-Toll and Dakar branches with a site-to-site IPsec VPN that guarantees confidentiality, integrity and availability.

Team

Environment
EVE-NG simulation
Deliverables
Report, 21-slide presentation
0%packet loss over 500 packets
2FortiGate firewalls
ESPencrypted traffic checked in Wireshark

The need

Two branches of the same company must exchange sensitive data over the Internet. They needed an encrypted, stable link that is easy to run, without a leased line.

Architecture

Each branch has its LAN behind a FortiGate. A Cisco router simulates the Internet between them, and the IPsec tunnel runs on top.

What I did

  1. Address both sites

    LAN 192.168.10.0/24 in Richard-Toll and 192.168.20.0/24 in Dakar, /30 WAN links to the Internet router.

  2. Route

    Static routing between the sites and towards router R2.

  3. Write the policies

    FortiGate security policies allowing only LAN-to-LAN traffic through the tunnel.

  4. Build the tunnel

    Site-to-site IPsec tunnel with the FortiGate wizard: IKE phase 1 with a pre-shared key, then phase 2.

  5. Test connectivity

    Pings and exchanges between the Windows clients of both branches.

  6. Prove the encryption

    Wireshark capture on the Internet link with the esp filter: the payload is no longer readable.

  7. Measure stability

    500 packets sent through the tunnel and a performance table.

Results

  • Both LANs talk as if they were on the same site.
  • Only encrypted ESP traffic is visible on the Internet link.
  • 0% loss over 500 packets.

Tools

FortiGate, IPsec, IKE, EVE-NG, Cisco IOS, Windows, Wireshark
