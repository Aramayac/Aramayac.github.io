A company network, designed then attacked

Design, deploy and audit a complete company infrastructure with security built in from the start, then measure every result.

The need

A small company needs a network that stays up when a device fails, keeps its public services apart from its internal network, and raises an alarm when it is attacked. The goal was to build that infrastructure end to end, then attack it myself to prove it holds.

Architecture

Three zones separated by a two-firewall cluster. Any flow not allowed by the flow matrix is denied and logged.

What I did

  1. Design before installing

    IPv4 plan split into /24, /26, /29 and /30, least-privilege flow matrix, logged default deny. Every phase could be rolled back with VMware snapshots.

  2. Make the firewall redundant

    Two pfSense firewalls in active/passive mode linked by CARP, pfsync and XMLRPC, with 7 virtual IPs. Failover tested by shutting down the master and by putting it into CARP maintenance mode.

  3. Segment the user network

    Four 802.1Q VLANs in /26 with DHCP, hardened trunks (native VLAN changed, allowed VLANs restricted) and per-service filtering rules.

  4. Centralise identities

    Active Directory on two replicated domain controllers, organisational units and GPOs: passwords, account and session lockout, USB blocking. Checked with repadmin and gpresult.

  5. Publish services in the DMZ

    Nginx over HTTPS and Postfix/Dovecot mail on Debian 13, with TLS certificates signed by an internal authority.

  6. Detect and block

    Suricata on both firewalls with the Emerging Threats ruleset and a custom threshold rule, automatic blocking of the attacker, and a pass list to cut false alarms.

  7. Monitor

    Zabbix 7.0 tracks 9 hosts through agents and SNMP, with triggers and email alerts tested end to end.

  8. Audit and fix

    NIST SP 800-115 approach with nmap, OWASP ZAP, Nikto, swaks, ncat and curl, then fixes: HSTS, CSP and security headers, stronger TLS, VRFY and ETRN disabled, open SMTP relay refused.

Problem solved on the way

A saturated link with no obvious cause

The traffic came from the Windows clients on the user network. I found the cause and neutralised it with a group policy, without touching the network core.

Results

  • Firewall failover measured between 3 and 5 seconds.
  • Account locked after 5 wrong passwords, as the GPO requires.
  • Port scan detected in 5 s and attacker blocked in 6 s.
  • Only 3 ports exposed after the audit.
  • Zero replication failures between the two domain controllers.
  • Zabbix alerting tested all the way to the inbox.
